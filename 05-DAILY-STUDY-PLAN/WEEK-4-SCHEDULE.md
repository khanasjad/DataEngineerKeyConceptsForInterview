## Day 22 (Monday): Interview Day + Tactical Prep

### Morning: Company-Specific Preparation

**Before each interview (1-2 hours):**
```
Deep Company Research:
1. Engineering blog (read 3-5 recent posts)
2. Tech stack (what they use, why)
3. Recent news (funding, product launches, layoffs)
4. Glassdoor reviews (interview process insights)
5. LinkedIn: Find your interviewers
   - Their background
   - What they work on
   - Connection points

Prepare tailored examples:
- How your Optum experience relates to their tech stack
- Questions showing you researched them
- Why you want to work there (specific reasons)

Review:
- Your resume (know every bullet cold)
- Your projects (ready to deep-dive)
- Your STAR stories (practice 1-2 most relevant)
```

### Interview (2-4 hours)

**During Interview:**
```
System Design:
- Ask clarifying questions (requirements, scale, constraints)
- Start with high-level architecture
- Discuss trade-offs ("We could use X or Y. X is better for... Y is better for...")
- Draw clear diagrams
- Estimate capacity
- Identify bottlenecks
- Propose optimizations

Coding:
- Clarify problem ("Should I handle edge case X?")
- Explain approach before coding
- Write clean, readable code
- Test with examples
- Discuss time/space complexity

Behavioral:
- Use STAR format (2-3 minutes per answer)
- Be specific with metrics
- Show leadership even if not manager
- Ask thoughtful questions

Your Questions to Ask:
- "What's the biggest data engineering challenge your team faces?"
- "How does the team balance technical debt vs new features?"
- "What does success look like in this role in first 6 months?"
- "Can you tell me about the team structure and collaboration?"
- "What technologies is the team excited about adopting?"
```

### Evening: Debrief + Prepare Next

**After interview (30 min):**
```
1. Send thank-you email:
   - Within 2-4 hours
   - Reference specific discussion points
   - Reiterate interest
   - Keep brief and professional

2. Document:
   - Questions asked
   - What went well
   - What to improve
   - Perceived outcome

3. Follow-up:
   - Update tracker
   - Note next steps
   - Set reminder for follow-up date
```

**Prep for tomorrow (1 hour):**
- Research next company
- Review relevant projects
- Light coding practice (stay sharp)

### Afternoon: Offer Negotiation Prep

**Prepare for offers (2 hours):**
```
Research Compensation:

1. Use Levels.fyi:
   - Search: "[Company] Data Engineer [Level]"
   - Note: Base, Bonus, Equity, Total Comp
   - Filter by location, YoE

2. Your target: $170k+ total comp
   - Sr Data Engineer: $150-200k
   - Staff Data Engineer: $180-250k
   - With GenAI/LLM skills: +20-40% premium

3. Know your minimum:
   - What's your walk-away number?
   - Current comp + 20% minimum?

Negotiation Framework:

When you get offer:
1. Express enthusiasm: "Thank you! I'm excited about this opportunity"
2. Ask for time: "Can I have 2-3 days to review?"
3. Don't accept immediately

Research & decide:
- Compare with market rates (Levels.fyi)
- Consider: Base, bonus, equity, benefits, growth
- Identify gap between offer and target

Negotiate:
- Email or phone (phone is better for complex negotiations)
- Be professional and collaborative
- "I'm very excited about joining [Company]. Based on my research and my skills in [GenAI/Kafka/Spark/etc], I was hoping for a total comp closer to $X. Is there flexibility here?"

Leverage:
- Other offers: "I have another offer at $X, but I prefer [your company] because..."
- Market data: "Based on Levels.fyi data for similar roles..."
- Your unique skills: "Given my experience with [GenAI/LLMs] which is in high demand..."

Never:
- Don't lie about other offers
- Don't be aggressive or entitled
- Don't accept first offer without negotiating (usually room for 10-20% increase)
```

## Days 29-30: Final Push

### Focus: Remaining Interviews + Decision Making

**Interview Intensity:**
- 2-4 interviews per day potentially
- Stay focused, don't burn out
- Each interview is independent

**Offer Management:**
```
When offers start coming:

1. Track all offers (spreadsheet)
2. Extend deadlines if needed:
   - "I'm in final rounds with [X], can you extend to [date]?"
3. Use competing offers as leverage:
   - "I have an offer from [Y] at $Z, but I'm more excited about [your company] because..."
4. Don't ghost companies
5. Be professional always

Decision framework:
- Does this meet your financial goal ($170k+)?
- Is the tech stack interesting?
- Will you learn and grow?
- Is the team strong?
- Is the culture a fit?
- Gut feeling?

When you decide:
- Accept verbally, get written offer
- Decline other offers professionally:
  - "Thank you for the opportunity. After careful consideration, I've decided to accept another offer that's a better fit for [reason]. I really enjoyed our conversations and hope we can stay in touch."
- Give notice (2 weeks standard)
```

**If no offers yet:**
- Don't panic (hiring takes time)
- Keep interviewing
- Follow up with recruiters
- Apply to more companies
- More mock interviews
- Review and improve

## Week 4 Pro Tips

**Energy Management:**
- Interviews are draining - take breaks
- Sleep well every night
- Eat healthy, stay hydrated
- Exercise to manage stress

**Performance:**
- Each interview is independent (don't let bad one affect next)
- Stay positive and confident
- Show enthusiasm (companies want people who want to be there)
- Be yourself (culture fit matters)

**Negotiation:**
- Everything is negotiable (salary, bonus, equity, start date, title)
- Be professional but advocate for yourself
- Don't be afraid to ask (worst they say is no)
- Get everything in writing

**Decision Making:**
- Don't rush (but respect timelines)
- Talk to trusted advisors
- Consider long-term career goals
- Trust your gut
